A Taste of the Course

A few questions to get you curious.

Tap any card to see where the science actually lands — or where it's still fiercely debated. These are just a handful of the puzzles we chase.

Can your hand reveal your sexual orientation?+
The ratio between your index and ring fingers — the 2D:4D ratio — is set in the womb and used as a rough proxy for prenatal testosterone. It's one of the field's most debated measures: linked in some studies to traits from spatial ability to athleticism, while bolder claims like sexual orientation remain hotly contested. We look at what the evidence actually supports.
Why grow ten pounds of antler just to fight over mates? Do humans hve something like that?+
Antlers, manes, and dazzling plumage are costly signals shaped by competition for mates — sexual selection, running right alongside natural selection. The same logic illuminates a surprising amount about human status displays, risk-taking, and body size. Darwin considered this some of his most provocative work.
Is there a body shape people find attractive everywhere?+
Decades of research on waist-to-hip ratio found preferences clustering near a specific range across many populations — proposed as a cue to health and fertility. But cross-cultural results are genuinely mixed, and how universal this really is remains an open fight. We read the original studies and the pushback side by side.
Why do men grow beards at all?+
Facial hair is a plausible sexually selected ornament — it enlarges the jaw and shifts perceptions of dominance and maturity. Yet whether beards actually raise attractiveness turns out to depend heavily on who you ask and how much stubble is involved. A perfect case study in how an "obvious" evolutionary story meets messy data.

what's the mystery behind women's orgasm? Does orgasm change the odds of conception?+
It's a surprisingly live research question. A 2024 study in the American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology examined whether orgasm relates to fecundability — the chance of conception in a given cycle. We walk through how researchers even design a test for something like this, and what the data currently suggest.

Why do "ideal partner age" preferences pull apart over a lifetime?+
Across many societies, men's preferred partner age skews progressively younger relative to their own as they age, while women's stays closer to their own. Evolutionary psychologists tie this to differences in reproductive biology — and it's one of the more consistently replicated patterns in the field, which makes it a great test case for evaluating these hypotheses.

…and this is barely the beginning. Fifteen weeks take us through jealousy, infidelity, gender, marriage, parenting, menopause, and much more.

Good to Know

A few things students ask.

Do I need a science background?

Not at all. The course builds every model from first principles, so students from any major can jump in and keep up. Some of the best discussions come from people bringing perspectives outside biology.

What makes this class different from a typical bio course?

You'll apply evolutionary logic directly to human behavior — attraction, jealousy, parenting, competition — rather than memorizing taxonomy or cell diagrams. The material is immediately relevant to your own life, and lecture discussions get genuinely lively.

Is this one of those huge lectures where nobody knows your name?

Lectures are large, but you also meet weekly in a small discussion section. That's where the real conversations happen, and where you get to know classmates well enough to form study groups.

How is the course graded?

Section participation, weekly problem sets, lecture quizzes, and exams. The structure is straightforward and spelled out on day one.

What do past students say?

It's consistently one of the most popular courses in the Anthropology department. Students regularly call it the class that changed how they think about themselves, relationships, and what it means to be human.
